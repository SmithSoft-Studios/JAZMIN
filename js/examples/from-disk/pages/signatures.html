Who published a file: verified publishers

A file can carry a publisher signature: which company or sole trader published it (one SmithSoft has checked), that not one byte has changed since, and the key facts signed with it, such as a payslip's period and net pay. The viewer shows it in its own bar, above the document, because a document's page could draw anything, a fake badge included.

A test build: nothing here is a real certification

These files are signed under the test root of spec/fixtures/publisher, whose keys are published so anyone can make test files. They open in a test build of the viewer (viewer-test/), which trusts that root and says TEST on every result. The released viewer trusts the roots SmithSoft publishes, which are built in before 1.8.0 is released: there these files show as signed by an unknown key (the last card).

What this shows

Eight signed files in the viewer: a certified payslip whose page is shown without the internet, a locked payslip whose publisher shows before the password, a sole trader, a co-signature, a withdrawn document, a changed one, one signed with a key nobody certified, and the payslip in the released viewer.

Feature: publisher signatures (1.8, spec 6.9) · USER-GUIDE §26

Verified publisher

A certified payslip

Sable Line Logistics' payroll signed it. The bar shows who, and the document's code; Details shows the registration number, when it was time-stamped, and the facts signed with it. The payslip's page asks for a web font: a certified document is sealed, shown without the internet, so what it shows can't change.

Verified publisher

Who sent it, before the password

A payslip locked with a password. The bar shows who published it before you type the password; the facts signed with it appear once the password opens it. Password: Sable-Payroll-2026!

Verified individual

A sole trader's invoice

SmithSoft certifies sole traders too: Thandi Mokoena, trading as TM Bookkeeping. The bar says Verified individual, and the invoice's number, buyer and total are its signed facts.

Co-signed

Signed, and co-signed by the auditor

Withdrawn

Withdrawn by its publisher

A payslip issued in error and withdrawn the next day. Its signature still holds, so the bar shows who published it, and that it was withdrawn: don't use it.

Not valid

Changed after it was signed

The certified payslip with one character changed afterwards: 12 hours' overtime became 72. The data still opens, and the bar says the file is not valid, so nobody is fooled by it.

Signed, not verified

Signed with a key nobody certified

Signed with a key its owner made, with "Sable Line Logistics" written in its own certificate. It hasn't changed since, but the viewer shows no name: anyone can write any name in a certificate of their own.

Signed, not verified

The same payslip in the released viewer

The released viewer trusts the roots SmithSoft publishes, not this test root: the payslip shows as signed by an unknown key, with no name, and its page is shown as any other (it may load its font).
